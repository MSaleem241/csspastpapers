📖 Understanding CSS Question Keywords

Each keyword signals a specific type of answer. Misreading the keyword is one of the most costly mistakes in CSS.

Critically Evaluate / Assess
Most demanding — requires judgment backed by evidence, not just description.
1Define key concepts and set evaluative criteria
2Present the dominant/mainstream view with evidence
3Identify weaknesses, contradictions, limitations
4Present counter-arguments and alternative perspectives
5Apply your criteria and reach a nuanced judgment
Analyze / Analyse
Break the topic into components and examine each in depth.
1Introduce with relevant context
2Break into key components or dimensions
3Examine each component with evidence
4Identify relationships, causes, effects
5Synthesize findings in a coherent conclusion
Discuss
Present both sides of an argument objectively, then reach a reasoned conclusion.
1Define key terms and scope
2Present arguments in favor (For)
3Present arguments against (Against)
4Support with examples and evidence
5Weigh both sides and offer balanced conclusion
Explain
Clarify how or why something works. Logical, step-by-step explanation.
1Define the concept or phenomenon clearly
2Provide historical or theoretical background
3Break explanation into logical sequential steps
4Use examples or analogies to illustrate
5Conclude with clear restatement
Compare
Identify similarities and differences between two or more subjects using clear criteria.
1Introduce both subjects and significance
2Establish comparison criteria
3Compare point by point (similarities)
4Contrast point by point (differences)
5Synthesize with overall comparative judgment
Examine
Look closely at a subject — describe its structure, consider evidence for and against.
1Identify the subject and establish context
2Describe structure, history, or components
3Consider different dimensions
4Identify strengths and evidence in favor
5Identify weaknesses and draw conclusions
